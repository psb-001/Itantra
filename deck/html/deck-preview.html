IDEA TITLE

1 · YOU SPEAK→2 · PHONE WRITES IT DOWN→ 3 · 129–278 BYTES→4 · OTHER PHONE SPEAKS IT measured on a real phone — 6.94 seconds of talking → a 211-byte message
Proposed Solution (Describe your Idea/Solution/Prototype)
What we built
  • Offline Android app — no internet, no cloud, no server
  • Speak → text on the phone → 129–278 bytes → spoken aloud on the other phone
  • Your voice is never sent and never recorded
  • Tested: works between two real Android phones
Detailed explanation of the proposed solution
How it actually works, step by step
  • Hold the button and talk · a tiny 629 KB listener waits for you to stop
  • A 340 MB speech model on the phone works out what you said
  • Anything written in the wrong letters is thrown away
  • The other phone reads it aloud — loud, and repeats until heard
How it addresses the problem
Why this solves the problem ISRO described
  • Voice is too big to send over a damaged radio
  • The words arrive and are then spoken aloud — so it reaches people who cannot read
  • It needs no network — so it works at the moment the network is what broke
ONE SENTENCE, THREE WAYS

66× smaller than a voice call · 1 050× smaller than the raw audio a mic records

FEASIBILITY AND VIABILITY

2 000 real recorded sentences · 6.72 hours of real human speech · all 10 languages · fewer wrong letters is better
Analysis of the feasibility of the idea
Proof this works — what we measured, and how
  • It works on two real phones — tested 27 Sep 2026. This had never worked before
  • Average 9 wrong letters in every 100 — 1 in 11 (CER 0.0918, word error 0.363)
  • Every language is under 14 wrong letters in 100 (all 10 under 0.14) · best Kannada 0.053, worst Odia 0.134
  • No internet used at any point — nothing recorded, nothing sent anywhere
  • Two parts are free to use but not for selling. We say so in plain language inside the app — see slide 6

Potential challenges and risks
What is still weak. We list it so you do not have to find it
What could go wrongThe honest position
Nobody has listened to the voicesEvery "how clear is it" number comes from a computer reading its own audio back — not a person listening
Tamil & Odia are weakAbout 1 word in 2 is wrong (word error 0.501 / 0.509)
3 of 10 languages stay silentGujarati, Kannada, Odia. We could not find a free, good-quality offline voice we were allowed to ship
Speed tested on a computer, not a cheap phoneA budget handset will be slower, by an amount we have not measured
Background noise was not realWe added noise to clean recordings afterwards — this is not a field test
The app file is 804 MBInstallable by hand, too big for an app store
Setting up two phones takes three manual stepsToo many for someone in a flood. Pairing happens at responder setup, not during the emergency; Wi-Fi Direct removes it for strangers; we are building in-app pairing to make it one tap
Strategies for overcoming these challenges
What we did about each one
  • We removed the characters it was getting wrong, rather than adjusting the model — so the mistake is now impossible, not just rarer
  • If a sentence is doubtful we drop the whole thing instead of sending a partly-fixed message. Silence can be retried; a confident wrong message cannot
  • Demo in English + Hindi, our two most accurate languages
  • We report wrong-letters and wrong-words — counting words alone flatters languages with long written words
  • Bluetooth first, Wi-Fi Direct as backup — we never rely on only one way of connecting
  • Only free open-source parts, and we say plainly what they do not allow

IMPACT AND BENEFITS

The network carries words. The phone handles the voice.
Potential impact on the target audience
Who uses this, and in what moment

Rescue teams

  • NDRF and SDRF rescue teams, district officials, camp volunteers
  • Responder and affected person are rarely in the same place
  • Because phones are deliberately paired, you know who sent an alert

Relief camps

  • Useful exactly when mobile data and internet are down
  • Runs on phones already in their pocket
  • No infrastructure to deploy

People who cannot read

  • Every other alert system needs you to read text — ours speaks it aloud
  • Regional first languages, uneven literacy
  • The group almost every alert system leaves out
Benefits of the solution (social, economic, environmental, etc.)
Social, money, and battery — plus what we can and cannot claim yet

Social

  • Voice in, voice out
  • Reaches people who cannot read or type
  • Inclusive by construction, not as an add-on

Economic

  • Nothing to pay for — no monthly service, no rented software
  • Runs on phones people already own
  • Two parts cannot be sold — fine for rescue use, not for a paid product

Environmental

  • A 211-byte message instead of ~14 000 bytes → 66× less data
  • Less radio energy per message
  • Battery effect not yet measured

✓ We have proved this

Works between two real phones · 2 000 real sentences across 10 languages · 66× less data than sending voice · works with no internet at all

✗ Not proved yet

Whether a person can actually understand the voices · speed on a cheap phone · battery life · how it behaves in a real disaster
Proposed outcomes, not proven results. We claim no lives saved and no guaranteed delivery.
